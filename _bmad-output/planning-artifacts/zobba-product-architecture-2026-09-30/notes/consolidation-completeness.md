## Consequential gaps still worth closing in the lead

1. **Recurring authority must survive operationally, not by impersonation.** Current lead §§4.3/6.4 define schedules and account custody but do not quite close the personal-account owner departure case. The concrete recommendation above supports both teams and sole practitioners: personal accounts remain valid for the solo case; a firm can use owned service/application accounts with explicit delegation. Scope and consent changes can block a Check even when its model/method is unchanged.
2. **A source request needs a durable definition of “enough”.** Lead rightly verifies population coverage but has no explicit product representation for several requested documents arriving over time. A lightweight request record connected to the same Task gives event-driven continuation a reliable purpose and prevents repeated reminders or premature completion.
3. **Technical acceptance currently dominates audit-quality acceptance.** Lead §8.6 is strong on deterministic state/scope failures. Retain an explicit separate audit-quality evaluation contract: synthetic engagements with known populations, omissions, ambiguous joins, conflicting criteria and supported findings; evaluate coverage, calculations, evidence support, correct uncertainty, useful investigation and reviewable output. This is an acceptance property, not another research prerequisite. The other audit-evaluation scope should integrate this centrally rather than leaving it in a test appendix.
4. **Exact processing and purchase choices should be consistent.** The lead selects hosted seats plus metered usage, while the alternate chooses tenant-paid provider keys. Keep the lead recommendation and make any optional BYOK explicit within the same model/destination policy; do not accidentally promise both billing defaults. Source/destination disclosure rules must apply to all providers regardless of who pays.
