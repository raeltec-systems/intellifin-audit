# Dots screenshot study: the visible working relationship

**Evidence:** four screenshots supplied by the user in the current conversation. **Scope:** visible product surfaces and interaction clues only. The screenshots are visible to this reviewer. This study neither verifies the product's release date nor claims access to its implementation. User, account, customer and record identifiers are deliberately not transcribed.

The useful reference is a continuing conversation with an assistant who is visibly doing work. Opening its computer changes how much of the work the user can see, while the same conversation remains available for instruction, clarification and delivery. At the time of this study, Zobba revision 2 supported that relationship. These findings now support the integrated revision 3. The main opportunity is to specify its everyday interaction in greater detail, preserving the Rust backend, fresh schema, continuing Task, real computer and standing Permissions.

## 2. The visible interaction pattern

The screenshots support the following reconstruction, with the limits above:

1. **A person delegates an outcome.** The assistant responds with a compact account of what it will investigate and what it intends to deliver. No procedure-authoring form is visible.
2. **Work and conversation coexist.** The person can keep reading, give direction and answer a question while the computer surface is open. They do not have to enter a separate run-monitoring product to watch it.
3. **The assistant starts from available access.** It describes the role already signed in, identifies the extra roles needed for coverage and proposes to finish useful work before switching. Access becomes a concrete next step in the task rather than a global onboarding detour.
4. **Authentication leaves the prose channel.** A contextual request leads to a dedicated sign-in surface. A compact receipt remains in the conversation afterward. The exact event recorded by the shown receipt is **details entered**, not verified authentication.
5. **A material contradiction becomes a small, well-framed question.** The assistant names the two conflicting rules and asks which governs this investigation. The user's short response is enough to continue the conversation; no blank requirements questionnaire is visible.
6. **Work is narrated at useful milestones.** Messages describe a reproduced problem, affected workflow, evidence collected and what will happen next. Those messages make the investigation legible, although still images cannot verify the claims.
7. **Authority is presented beside the affected action.** The upload request identifies its destination and offers a one-time or persistent choice, with technical parameters behind disclosure. The standing-choice affordance is visible; its exact security contract is unknown.
8. **The result remains discoverable.** The header output link and the assistant card's Outputs list provide ways to revisit work after it has scrolled out of the conversation.

The appeal is continuity: the user can move between delegation, watching, answering, private access and checking a result without feeling that the job has been abandoned and restarted at each step. An ongoing assistant identity reinforces this, but client boundaries and accountable people remain necessary in Zobba.

## 3. Concrete refinements to the lead Zobba design

These findings originally refined revision 2's product surface and now inform revision 3. Section anchors below describe that original review baseline; use the [current lead design](../../Zobba-Product-and-Architecture-Design.md) for the integrated recommendation.

| Refinement | Recommended Zobba behavior | Existing design anchor |
|---|---|---|
| **Make the task a continuing working relationship** | Keep the task conversation usable through work cycles, waiting, corrections and later evidence. Show Zobba's stable identity plus the current engagement and Task. Returning to a task restores what the person was inspecting. Never turn a continuing assistant identity into an unscoped conversation across client engagements. | §§1.1–1.3, 3.4 |
| **Use a compact task companion panel** | From the task header, open a panel for current work, outstanding input, computer/account/environment, work products and Permissions. Include a computer thumbnail only when viewing is authorised and current; replace it with privacy, stopped or reconnecting status when appropriate. Keep method/skill/context details one step away rather than always occupying the workspace. | §§1.2, 2.1, 3.4 |
| **Open the work, preserve the conversation** | A progress or output card opens Computer, Data or Documents alongside the same conversation. Close returns to the spacious conversation. Expand and Pin remain predictable; an inspection is not replaced by incoming work unless the user has chosen Follow Zobba. Remember the person's reading position. | §§1.2, 1.3 |
| **Separate human attention from task activity** | A request says what needs a person, who can answer, which part is waiting and what continues: “Needs your sign-in · continuing analysis of the supplied population.” Display a direct action in the request; keep a compact outstanding-input item when it scrolls away. Show “Waiting for sign-in” when nothing independent remains. Do not make one whole-task spinner carry all of these meanings. | §§2.1–2.3, 8.4 |
| **Give questions enough context to be answered briefly** | State the discrepancy, cite the relevant sources/versions, say what decision changes and recommend a choice when supported. Offer concise choices and free text. A short reply binds to the identified question; display the accepted meaning. Record who decided and what work is affected. A task reply cannot override required firm methodology or fabricate a review approval; a necessary methodology change belongs to Admin's ordinary Save. | §§1.3, 3.2–3.4 |
| **Make receipt language exact** | Guidance uses Received and Applied as already designed. Private sign-in uses Waiting for sign-in → Details submitted or Handed back → Verifying access → Signed in as [role/account] or Needs attention. A receipt stores no credentials and does not mark access usable until the account/application check succeeds. Read/seen timestamps must not masquerade as Applied. | §§2.2–2.3, 6.4 |
| **Make application-role changes explicit** | The access request names the application, verified environment, requested role and purpose. After handback, show the verified active role before dependent work resumes. A next role is another scoped handoff, not silent reuse of whoever happens to be signed in. Keep the independent-review distinction clear: logging in as an application manager is not Zobba audit sign-off. | §§2.3–2.4, 3.1, 6.4 |
| **Keep protected sign-in visually separate and contextually close** | An inline request opens a focused private session carrying a verified origin, required role, privacy status and clear return path. Keep Take over to sign in as the standard full application path, including MFA. Offer credential-entry convenience only for a qualified trusted adapter with the same isolation guarantees; do not promise generic website password injection. Not now records a durable wait and states which work can continue. Other viewers see the privacy cover. | §§2.3, 7.2, 8.2 |
| **Make one-time and standing authority understandable** | When standing Permissions do not cover an action, show the destination, environment, account/role, purpose, exact material and effect. Offer **Allow this action** and, where policy permits, **Set a standing rule**. The latter opens a compact, bounded rule with scope/expiry/limits and visible revocation. Use existing standing authority silently for routine permitted work and show its basis on inspection. Avoid a bare domain-wide Always allow promise. | §§3.1–3.2 |
| **Narrate meaningful progress without flooding the task** | Report a discovered issue, coverage achieved, changed approach, material uncertainty or upcoming access dependency. Link each claim to its work product or evidence when available. Group low-level actions in Activity. Use the named task activity rather than a persistent generic Thinking overlay that hides conversation text. | §§1.2–1.3, 2.1 |
| **Provide a return-to-work digest** | When the user returns, show changes since their last visit: work completed, supported findings, questions, products ready and any remaining limitations. Distinguish continued work, durable waiting and stopped work. This is a Zobba recommendation derived from the desired away-work relationship; away-work is not demonstrated in the screenshots. | §§1.3, 2.1, 8.4 |
| **Give outputs a stable home with truthful review labels** | Maintain a task work-product shelf with title, type, latest version, state, supporting coverage and review attribution. Chat and header links open that same object. Show Draft, Ready for team review, Independently reviewed, or Self-reviewed as applicable; retain issued versions. Generated is not reviewed, and application approval testing is not an audit review. | §§1.1–1.3, 2.4, 4.2 |

These details should be expressed in a few concrete reference journeys rather than a proliferation of settings screens: first delegated task, private role handoff, a consequential question while work continues, a request beyond standing Permissions, return after time away, and reviewing a supported output.

## 4. Example Zobba journey using these patterns

**Objective:** “Check whether terminated users lost access on time for this quarter. Investigate the exceptions and prepare the working paper.”

Zobba acknowledges the intended result and shows a compact Working brief with engagement, period, methodology and expected work product. It begins authorised population acquisition and opens a relevant data view only if following is enabled.

It needs application access for selected case inspection: “Sign in to the access system as the engagement's read-only auditor. I can continue matching the supplied records while you do that.” The request names the verified environment and opens the private sign-in surface. After handback, its status is Verifying access until the intended identity and access are confirmed. The computer badge then names the active role and who controls it.

A conflict emerges: “The saved engagement decision uses the payroll closing date; the applicable policy uses the contractual termination date. This changes which cases exceed the time limit. I recommend the policy date. Use that for this task?” The relevant sources and versions are inspectable. If the auditor has authority to resolve that engagement criterion, the answer is recorded once and applied to affected work. If the request would alter required methodology, Zobba identifies that dependency and routes the configuration edit to Admin; Admin uses ordinary Save.

The person can ask “Show the exceptions with incomplete evidence” while independent matching continues. The results open beside the conversation and stay in place until the person closes them or resumes following. A milestone says what is supported, what is inconclusive and what the next step requires.

When the person returns, the task shows what changed and any remaining input. Its working paper appears in Work products with its coverage and exact review state. A team engagement goes to the assigned eligible reviewer. A permitted solo engagement labels the result Self-reviewed. Neither path pretends that the assistant, or a role in the audited application, supplied independent audit sign-off.

## 5. What not to infer or copy literally

- No screenshot establishes background durability, latency, resumability, model architecture, memory implementation, credential protection, operating system, permission enforcement or multi-agent internals. The displayed browser/desktop surface does not reveal how it is hosted or streamed.
- “Thinking”, “Active”, “Connected” and a credentials-entered receipt are different kinds of signal. Zobba should present each in terms of the fact it actually knows.
- The screenshot's policy question is a useful interaction pattern; it is not evidence that the underlying criterion selection was correct or that chat direction can override mandatory controls.
- A broad Always allow label is concise but under-specified for audit work. Zobba should keep the low-friction choice while making the standing scope inspectable and bounded.
- The named assistant persona can create continuity without copying OpenAI's avatar, artwork or product identity. Keep Pair and Zobba's visual direction. Place status where it does not overlap readable work.
- Call and Slack are visible entry points, not evidence of their detailed flows. The screenshots do not justify making them dependencies of Zobba's first complete experience.
- No independent audit review, self-review, recurring-method setup, methodology administration or issued-evidence flow is shown. Zobba's specialist behavior for these must follow the lead design rather than an imagined Dots feature.
